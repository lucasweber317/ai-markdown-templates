# Assumptions

> Durable assumptions that influence how interviews are read and synthesized.
> Avoid adding temporary notes. Only record assumptions likely to remain relevant over time.

---

## Active Assumptions

**Instructions:** Add assumptions here that are stable and will shape how multiple syntheses are read over time. Each assumption should be specific enough to guide interpretation but general enough to remain relevant for months.

**Examples of good assumptions:**
- Existing customers solve the secondary themes upstream, so direct demand for them is low
- Most accounts in this segment standardize on a single collaboration platform, so integration pain reads as platform-specific
- Champions in this segment overstate urgency; corroborate stated priority against the allocation exercise before treating a theme as hot
- Smaller accounts rarely surface compliance themes because procurement, not the user, owns that requirement

**Examples of assumptions to avoid:**
- ❌ "Interview with Acme is scheduled for next week" (timing, not an assumption)
- ❌ "Users want a better product" (too vague, not actionable)
- ❌ "Contact at Acme prefers Slack" (specific to one interview, not durable)

**Tips for writing good assumptions:**
- Start from validated signal (recurring across interviews, allocation data, market context)
- Make them falsifiable — a future interview could prove them wrong
- Focus on how a segment behaves or how signal should be weighed, not on a single conversation
- Update or remove assumptions when interviews contradict them
